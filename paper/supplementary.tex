\section{Agent and Retrieval Stack}
\label{sec:agent}

\stitle{Search agent.}
The agent follows a ReAct-style loop: at each turn it emits either a search
action (a query wrapped in \texttt{<search>} tags) or a final answer
(wrapped in \texttt{<answer>} tags).
Each search returns the top-3 documents (title and abstract text) from the
fixed corpus, appended to the context as an observation block.
Episodes allow at most 4 search turns; decoding is greedy
(temperature $0$) with stop sequences on the action delimiters and on
generation of an observation-opening tag, so that the model cannot hallucinate
its own retrieval results.
Unclosed action tags at end-of-generation are parsed conservatively
(an unclosed \texttt{<search>}/\texttt{<answer>} span is accepted only when it
is unambiguous; occurrences are counted and audited).
A same-code control comparison between parser versions showed no evidence of
a behavioral effect of these parsing safeguards, and no safeguard-specific
mechanism activated in the confirmatory runs.

\stitle{Corpus.}
All settings use the official HotpotQA \texttt{enwiki-20171001} abstract
corpus (5{,}233{,}235 documents).
2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue are evaluated against the same fixed corpus;
annotated supporting documents are covered for 92.7\% of 2WikiMultiHopQA and
98.0\% of MuSiQue validation questions, and confirmatory 2WikiMultiHopQA
evaluation is restricted to covered questions (main paper, Sec.~4.1).

\stitle{Retrievers.}
Lexical retrieval uses BM25 (\texttt{bm25s}).
Dense retrieval uses \texttt{intfloat/e5-base-v2} with brute-force inner
product over the full corpus (fp32).
The relevance baseline is the MS~MARCO cross-encoder
\texttt{cross-encoder/}\allowbreak\texttt{ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2},
scoring each candidate's title and text against the current query $q_t$.
All retriever configurations are fixed across the five evaluation settings.

\section{Candidate Families}
\label{sec:candidates}

For every eligible state, the retrieval pool $\mathcal{C}_t$ is the union of
BM25 top-20 lists under $q_t$, $q_0$, and up to six already-seen document
titles (entity-style queries), plus dense top-20 lists under $q_t$ and $q_0$,
excluding all previously consumed documents
($109.1\pm27.7$ documents per state in the primary evaluation).
Up to eight typed candidates are drawn per state:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.15em
  \item \textbf{bm25\_top} --- BM25 top-1 under $q_t$;
  \item \textbf{dense\_top} --- dense top-1 under $q_t$;
  \item \textbf{ce\_top} --- cross-encoder argmax over $\mathcal{C}_t$
        (the relevance-selection baseline);
  \item \textbf{mid\_rel} --- a BM25 rank 9--16 document under $q_t$;
  \item \textbf{novelty} --- in the pool but outside BM25 top-20 under $q_t$
        and outside dense top-5 (low current-query relevance, reached via
        $q_0$/dense-tail expansion);
  \item \textbf{redundancy} --- high $q_t$ relevance (BM25 top-10) with the
        highest lexical (Jaccard) overlap against already-seen evidence
        (retained only if overlap $>0.05$);
  \item \textbf{entity\_bridge} --- reached only via entity-title queries and
        absent from the BM25 ranking under $q_t$;
  \item \textbf{random} --- a random remaining pool member (control).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{Consistency of the CE baseline.}
The \textbf{ce\_top} family is constructed so that the CE argmax over the full
pool $\mathcal{C}_t$ is always contained in the retained intervention set
$\mathcal{D}_t$: candidates are drawn without replacement, and if the
pool-level CE argmax has already been taken as \textbf{bm25\_top} or
\textbf{dense\_top}, it remains in $\mathcal{D}_t$ under that label while
\textbf{ce\_top} takes the next-ranked document.
Consequently, $\arg\max_{d\in\mathcal{D}_t} R_{\mathrm{CE}}(q_t,d)$ and
$\arg\max_{d\in\mathcal{C}_t} R_{\mathrm{CE}}(q_t,d)$ select the same document,
and the main paper's $d_{\mathrm{CE}}$ (defined over $\mathcal{D}_t$) equals
the pool-level CE selection.
We verified this empirically: in all 3{,}881 evaluated states across the five
settings, the pool-level CE argmax is present in $\mathcal{D}_t$
(zero exceptions).

Standard-retrieval-only analyses restrict the oracle to
\{bm25\_top, dense\_top, ce\_top\}; leave-one-family-out analyses remove one
family at a time (main paper, Sec.~5.5).

\section{Preregistration, Lockbox, and Provenance}
\label{sec:prereg}

\stitle{Preregistration chain.}
All confirmatory decisions were fixed in a version-controlled design-revisions
document \emph{before} the corresponding data were inspected, including:
the preference threshold $\tau=0.1$ with sensitivity values fixed in advance;
the primary Gate-2 endpoints; the lockbox endpoints (H1--H3) and the analysis
freeze list; the predictor judgment bands and the pre-committed decision to
stop the predictor line if strict criteria were not met (they were not; the
result is reported as a negative finding rather than retried);
the replication protocols for 2WikiMultiHopQA, the cross-scale agent, and
MuSiQue; and the final review-defense analyses (cross-fitted oracle,
question-clustered inference, pool-robustness, and effect-size
decomposition).
No acceptance criterion was relaxed after unblinding at any point;
criteria that failed remain recorded as failures together with the
adjudication that followed.

\stitle{Single-open lockbox.}
The held-out HotpotQA lockbox was sealed before predictor development:
question IDs were selected and committed, all models to be evaluated were
frozen to disk, and SHA-256 hashes of the frozen artifacts and the design
document were recorded in a provenance file within the repository before
opening.
The evaluation script refuses to run if a lockbox report already exists, so
the lockbox was evaluated exactly once.

\stitle{Measurement-integrity control.}
A preregistered harness audit compared agent behavior across parser versions.
The strict no-op criterion (99\% identical trajectories) was \emph{not} met
(94\%); a preregistered same-code control (re-running the identical legacy
code twice under production conditions) yielded 76\% identical trajectories,
attributing the discrepancy to serving-level nondeterminism, and zero
activations of the new parsing mechanisms were observed in confirmatory runs.
We therefore report ``no evidence of a behavioral effect'' rather than a
proven no-op, and record the original criterion as failed.

\section{Pairwise Label Construction}
\label{sec:labels}

For candidates $d_i, d_j$ at the same state, the preference label is decided
by the difference in \emph{raw} intervention outcomes (downstream answer F1)
with a preregistered tie zone $\tau=0.1$: pairs with $|U_i-U_j|\le\tau$ are
ties and are excluded from accuracy denominators.
Labels therefore never depend on the matched-control arms, consistent with the
note on control matching in Sec.~\ref{sec:case}.
Label reliability on the replay-audited subset is 97.8--99.0\% (three greedy
continuations per intervention on 20\% of states; 115/579 primary states and
137/686 lockbox states).
The distribution of preference strengths is bimodal (most decided pairs are
either near-ties or large-margin), and threshold sensitivity at
$\tau\in\{0.05, 0.2\}$ preserves the direction of all decided comparisons.
Pair representations used by learned models are constructed to be
orientation-invariant (candidates serialized in canonical order;
magnitude-based pair features), which removes label-orientation leakage; an
early signed-difference variant was discarded for this reason before any
confirmatory use.

\section{State-Delta Feature Definitions}
\label{sec:features}

The interpretable feature vector $\varphi(s_t,d)$ asks what the candidate
\emph{adds} relative to current knowledge.
Content tokens are lowercased alphanumeric tokens with a fixed stopword list
removed; entity proxies are capitalized tokens and multi-digit numbers.
With $D$ the candidate's content-token set, $E_1,\dots,E_k$ the per-document
evidence token sets ($E=\bigcup_i E_i$), $Q$ and $Q_t$ the token sets of the
original question and current query:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.15em
  \item \textbf{novelty} $=|D\setminus E|/|D|$ --- fraction of candidate
        content unseen in evidence;
  \item \textbf{redundancy} $=\max_i \mathrm{Jaccard}(D,E_i)$ --- maximal
        overlap with any single consumed document;
  \item \textbf{entity\_gain} --- new entity-proxy types per 100 tokens
        (scale-clipped);
  \item \textbf{query\_align} $=|D\cap Q_t|/|Q_t|$;
  \item \textbf{q0\_align} $=|D\cap Q|/|Q|$;
  \item \textbf{gap\_coverage} $=|D\cap(Q\setminus E)|/|Q\setminus E|$ ---
        coverage of question tokens not yet present in evidence;
  \item \textbf{doc\_len} $=\log(1+\text{token count})$, normalized;
  \item \textbf{turn\_idx}, \textbf{n\_evidence} --- search step and number of
        consumed documents, normalized.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Semantic variants of alignment/novelty features (E5 embedding based) were
additionally used in the gate campaign (Sec.~\ref{sec:secondary}).
The refit coefficients reported in the main paper (Fig.~3; Table~S2) are logistic
models without intercept over these fixed-scale normalized features (no
z-scoring is applied) within CE-conflicting pairs.

\section{Case Study: Full Trajectories}
\label{sec:case}

The main-paper case (Table~1 there) is lockbox state
\texttt{5ae5d06a\ldots::s1}.
\emph{Question:} ``From which team did the linebacker for four NFL teams
between 1985--1996 get fired by on November 29?'' (gold: the Jacksonville
Jaguars).
\emph{State $s_1$:} the agent's first search
(``linebacker who played for four nfl teams between 1985-1996'') returned
\emph{Jack Del Rio} (``head coach of the Oakland Raiders \ldots A
linebacker for four NFL teams between 1985--1996''), \emph{Dan Brandenburg},
and \emph{Fredd Young}.
The agent's visible reasoning at this point reads: ``Jack Del Rio is the
only one who fits the criteria \ldots I need to find out which team fired
him on November 29.''

\stitle{Base continuation (no intervention).}
Four further searches (``Jack Del Rio fired by on November 29'', ``\ldots
fired date'', ``\ldots fired by which team \ldots'') fail to surface the
2011 Jaguars firing; the agent answers ``Oakland Raiders'' (F1 0).

\stitle{CE-selected candidate (pool CE rank 1): \emph{Wilber Marshall}}
(60 tokens; ``\ldots was a linebacker in the National Football League (NFL)
for five teams from 1984 until 1995 \ldots'').
Continuation: the agent immediately searches ``Wilber Marshall fired on
November 29'' and answers ``Dallas Cowboys'' (F1 0).
The document is the most query-aligned candidate in the pool but concerns a
different entity; it displaces a correct hypothesis.

\stitle{Useful candidate (pool CE rank 15; \emph{redundancy} family;
non-gold): \emph{Kevin Hardy (linebacker)}}
(70 tokens; ``\ldots he played professionally for the Jacksonville Jaguars,
Dallas Cowboys, and Cincinnati Bengals of the NFL'').
Continuation: the agent keeps the Del Rio hypothesis, issues one search
(``Jack Del Rio fired by on November 29''), and answers ``Jacksonville
Jaguars'' (F1 1).
Interpretable features: query alignment 0.50 (identical to the CE-selected
document's 0.50), novelty 0.51, redundancy 0.35.

\stitle{Matched control (\emph{Fredd Young}; same BM25 rank bucket, 63
tokens).}
Continuation reproduces the base failure pattern (four searches, wrong
answer), so the useful candidate's effect is not attributable to generic
context expansion.
(This control happens to be a document already present in the context; see
the note on control matching below.)

\stitle{Caveats.}
PIU records the realized downstream effect: the agent's visible reasoning
never cites the injected Kevin Hardy document, so the mechanism is the
availability of the missing entity in context rather than explicit
citation.
This state is outside the 20\% replay-audited subset (single greedy
continuation per arm).
The case was selected from lockbox states satisfying (i) base and
CE-selected outcomes F1 $<0.3$, (ii) a non-gold, non-CE-top candidate with
F1 $\ge0.9$ and pool CE rank $\ge4$; 25 states met these criteria and this
one was chosen for interpretability of the mechanism.

\stitle{Note on control matching.}
Matched controls are drawn from the BM25 top-100 under $q_t$ subject to the
rank-bucket, length, title-overlap, and non-gold constraints; documents
already present in the agent's context are \emph{not} excluded by the
matching code.
This occurs for 8.4--10.0\% of controls across settings (lockbox: 648 of
7{,}120) and amounts to a near-duplicate injection, i.e., a conservative
control.
All confirmatory endpoints (opportunity, Oracle--CE headroom, pairwise
labels, specialist and gate evaluations) are computed from the raw
intervention outcome $Y(s_t,E_{<t}\cup\{d\})$ and are unaffected;
controlled PIU is a diagnostic quantity.

\stitle{Exchangeability check: CE is a serious baseline.}
Under a null in which the CE choice is exchangeable with a random pool member,
the oracle$-$random gain on the lockbox would be $+0.155$
(95\% null interval $[0.141, 0.169]$), whereas the observed oracle$-$CE gain
is $+0.117$.
CE selection is therefore significantly better than random selection within
the same pools; the reported headroom is measured \emph{beyond} a genuinely
informative baseline.

\stitle{Gate and predictor campaign summary.}
Deployment-time models were developed strictly on development data and frozen
before lockbox evaluation.
Standalone pairwise utility prediction reached 57.0\% (v1) and at most 59.3\%
(v2) accuracy; on CE-disagreement pairs it remained below chance (39--47\%),
while CE relevance alone reached 53.1\% and CE combined with measured-utility
features 62.1\%.
For explicit CE-conflict identification, four gate families of increasing
capacity (lexical/retrieval statistics; CE-score distribution and probe
signals; semantic state--candidate representations; a fine-tuned transformer
gate) converged to development AUC 0.61--0.69; the frozen ensemble achieved
0.641 on the lockbox, and the resulting deployable mixture recovered
approximately none of the oracle-condition routing advantage
(recovery $\approx -0.02$; main paper Fig.~4).
Oracle-condition routing itself is stable at 71--76\% across all five
settings (76.0 / 76.2 / 71.2 / 75.2 / 71.7\%).

\stitle{Gold is not sufficient either.}
Among gold-labeled candidate documents in the main Gate-1 analysis, 74.2\% did not
improve the downstream outcome at their state (62.9\% in the preregistered
Gate-2 sample); in a weakened-agent stress condition this fell to 37.5\%,
showing that even ground-truth supporting evidence has state-dependent
utility.

\stitle{Context pollution.}
Omission interventions (I1) show that removing already-consumed non-gold
evidence improves downstream answer F1 by $+0.057$ on average ($n=330$
omission arms), while removing gold evidence harms performance more than
removing non-gold evidence --- an independent sanity check of the measurement
and complementary evidence that additional relevant-looking context is not
uniformly beneficial.

\stitle{Search-termination utility.}
Interventions can change search behavior itself: the fraction of continuations
that terminate search immediately after the intervention rises from 7.1\% to
12.9\% under the stress condition, indicating that part of a document's value
lies in enabling the agent to stop searching.

\stitle{Where opportunities live.}
Opportunities concentrate in base-failure states (37.5\% of failure states vs.\
0.8\% of success states in the development analysis), motivating the
failure-enriched confirmatory sampling; the representative (unenriched)
development sample retains an 18.5\% opportunity rate.

\stitle{Cost accounting.}
96.2\% of rollout token cost is prompt tokens (context replay), implying that
amortized approximations of prospective intervention should target context
reuse rather than generation length.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

\stitle{Software and hardware.}
vLLM 0.10.2, PyTorch 2.8.0 (CUDA 12.8), Transformers 4.55.2,
\texttt{bm25s} 0.3.10, scikit-learn 1.9.0, Python 3.11; NVIDIA H100 80GB.
Greedy decoding throughout; fixed seeds for sampling, stratification, and
control matching.

\stitle{Scale of the study.}
Per-setting confirmatory sizes (questions\,/\,states\,/\,conflicting pairs): HotpotQA dev 297\,/\,579\,/\,---; lockbox 350\,/\,686\,/\,1{,}639;
2Wiki 350\,/\,819\,/\,1{,}684; Qwen2.5-14B 350\,/\,838\,/\,1{,}930;
MuSiQue 349\,/\,959\,/\,2{,}014.
The cross-fitted oracle uses 150 fresh questions with two independent rollout
sets.

\stitle{Code and records.}
Anonymized code, the preregistration document, and frozen per-gate reports are
available at the repository linked in the abstract.
All confirmatory numbers in the main paper trace to a preregistered criterion
and a frozen report file in that repository.
